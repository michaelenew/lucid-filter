# 0037/0038 — the shed is removable; the (phi,s) bank does NOT retire the family on hot regimes

> **⚖️ ATTRIBUTION —** _Two results: removing the hand-built shed (a changepoint detector) in favour of inferring the scale by likelihood under the class (φ,s); and a negative result that a (φ,s) model-averaging bank (MMAE/IMM over the "sloppy ridge") does not retire the family on hot regimes — the calm-dominated likelihood identifies s as small, and forgetting is not the fix._ Prior art: multiple-model adaptive estimation — Magill 1965; interacting multiple model — Blom & Bar-Shalom 1988; Bayesian model averaging. Status: REPRODUCTION (MMAE bank) with NEGATIVE-RESULT.

## Shed removed (works)

optimality-proof Prop 1 is exactly the sensor-glitch-vs-level-jump confound, resolved by making
`(phi, s)` the class definition and inferring the scale by likelihood -- "no thresholds, no
changepoint detection." My shed was a hand-built changepoint detector. Removed entirely; the derived
robust-MAP (0031) already reacts at the first sample, the walk follows. Cost only ~0.1x on the hot
regimes (pot-hot 1.13, process+pot 1.18 vs shed 1.03/1.15). The filter now has **no tuning
parameters** -- only the class `(phi, s)` and labeled-budget timescales.

## The (phi,s) bank does not retire the family here -- forget is not the lever

Findings 13-16 retire even the `(phi,s)` *point* by model-averaging a bank over the sloppy ridge,
because tracking is *flat along the ridge*. `AdaptiveBank` implements it (1-member bank == the single
filter exactly). But on the hot regimes it sits at pot-hot **1.36**, process+pot **1.58** -- worse
than a single good member.

**The remedy (forget) fails.** `forget = 1.0` and `0.99` are identical (1.359); aggressive `forget
= 0.8` helps only marginally (1.36 -> 1.24) and plateaus. The per-step likelihood is dominated by
the long calm stretches, so the bank concentrates on the calm-optimal member regardless of
forgetting.

**Why: the ridge is NOT flat on the hot regimes.** Single-member ratio across the grid (4 seeds):

| pot-hot | s=0.3 | s=0.5 | s=0.8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| phi=0.70 | 1.10 | 1.04 | 1.05 |
| phi=0.85 | 1.21 | 1.10 | 1.06 |
| phi=0.90 | 1.28 | 1.13 | 1.07 |
| phi=0.95 | 1.40 | 1.19 | 1.08 |

A strong gradient in `s`: a **large-s member (0.8) does great (1.04-1.08, beating the shed's 1.03)**;
small-s under-reaches (1.40). The shed was compensating for a too-small `s`. The bank's calm-dominated
likelihood identifies `s` as *small* (calm has little scale variation), so it under-reaches the rare
large bursts. The flat-ridge premise (findings 13) holds for normal regimes but breaks in the extreme
failing-sensor regimes -- where `s` is exactly the axis that matters and the bursts that pin it large
are rare.

## What this points to (open)

Two candidate fixes, not yet realized:
1. The reach should come from a **within-member scale posterior** (the 0008 windowed GPB1) that opens
   on a burst regardless of the member's `s`. My quick sigma-point port (0038) blows up (pot-hot
   ~4.9x) -- a broken realization, kept as a negative result; its reach is the finding-18 analogue
   `q`, an open derivation.
2. Or `s` for a burst-prone system genuinely is large, and it is under-identified by mostly-calm
   data -- which is the parent's flagged-open "learn the shape online / drop the Gaussian-AR(1)"
   item (a rare-large log-scale is heavy-tailed, not a single-timescale AR(1)).

Status: shed removed and validated (no tuning parameters, ~0.1x cost); the ridge-integral over the
family is understood but does NOT retire it on the hot regimes, and forget is not the fix.
